Where Muon’s Advantage Lives

Small-scale causal localization of an optimizer’s effect on learned features

Maxwell Twelftree, David Lemphers, Zeyu Zhang & Yue Yang

Maincode, Melbourne, Australia · max@maincode.com

Paper Code & logs Poster Talk (soon) Cite

In short

We trained the same 124M language model with two optimizers, Muon and AdamW, and compared what they learned. The features agree across optimizers about as well as AdamW agrees with itself across random seeds. Muon’s training gain, and its more reproducible features, come almost entirely from one place: the attention value circuit, WVWO.

§1The question

Muon replaces each momentum update with its nearest orthogonal matrix. It trains language models faster than AdamW, and it leaves a visible fingerprint on the weights: flatter singular spectra and higher stable rank.

Muon, in one line With momentum M = UΣVT, step along W ← W − η·UVT. Every singular value of the step is set to 1, so no direction dominates.

That raises two questions. Does the optimizer change which features a model learns, or only how it stores them? And if Muon helps, where inside a transformer does the useful part act?

§2Comparing features across models

We train an identical top-k sparse autoencoder on layer 6 of every model (12,288 features, k = 32). Their decoder directions don’t line up, even between two seeds of the same optimizer (mean best cosine 0.14), so we compare behaviour instead: two features match if they fire on the same tokens.

Averaging that score over live features gives a feature match between two models. Two seeds of the same optimizer set the ceiling; the same score with tokens misaligned is 0.024. All comparisons are at matched validation loss (Muon step 4,375, AdamW step 5,100).

§3The features match, at the seed ceiling

A careful claim This is non-inferiority to the AdamW seed ceiling under one protocol (top-k SAEs, firing-pattern matching), not a claim that the two optimizers share an optimizer-independent set of features.

The geometry is different all the same. Muon’s weight stable ranks are 1.6–5.3× AdamW’s and its residual stream is 5–7× larger. What it buys is reproducibility: Muon lands on more nearly the same features from seed to seed, and that gap opens early.

§4Where the advantage lives

An attention head factors into two circuits: a scoring circuit WQWKT that decides where to look, and a value circuit WVWO that decides what gets written. We keep the architecture and data fixed and give Muon only some of the matrices, AdamW the rest. Try it: each switch below is a real training arm (three seeds).

The baseline. Hand a circuit to Muon to see what it buys.

  • Sufficient. Muon on WVWO alone recovers 86% of the loss gap and gives the most reproducible features of any arm (0.632).
  • Necessary for the loss. Muon on everything except WVWO recovers none of the loss gain (3.369, slightly behind AdamW).
  • A passenger. Muon on WQWK triples the scoring circuit’s stable rank (6.7 → 21.0) and buys nothing.

§6Scope

This is a small-scale study by design: most causal runs are 124M parameters with three seeds, with checks at 350M; one SAE family (top-k, firing-pattern matching); one dataset (FineWeb, 2.67B tokens). The value-circuit result is sufficiency plus loss-level necessity at 124M. Whether a selective-Muon recipe pays off in wall-clock at scale is open.

Every run’s log, the matching outputs and the scripts are in the repository.
